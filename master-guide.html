5 Evergreen Anchor Posts

Post one every 4–7 days. No hashtag overload. Let silence work. Don't explain in comments.

Post 1 — Transitions (Foundational · Pin this)

Most people don't change because they suddenly know what they want. They change because something quietly stops fitting. That moment — when the old way no longer works but the new way isn't clear yet — is not confusion. It's transition. Clarity often disappears before direction appears. That doesn't mean something is wrong. It means a chapter is completing.

Post 2 — Timing (Authority Signal)

Not every phase of life is meant for action. Some phases are meant for: · understanding · consolidation · integration · letting go When we try to force movement during a reflective phase, even good decisions feel heavy. Timing matters more than effort — and recognizing timing is often the difference between strain and alignment.

Post 3 — Decisions (Trust Builder)

When people say they're "stuck," what they usually mean is this: They're trying to make a decision without context. Without understanding the phase they're in, decisions feel urgent, pressured, or overwhelming. Context doesn't tell you what to choose. It tells you how to choose — and whether now is the moment to act or wait.

Post 4 — Success & Misalignment (Depth)

One of the most misunderstood moments in life is this: You've done everything right — and yet something still feels off. That doesn't mean you failed. It often means you've outgrown the structure that once supported you. Success doesn't exempt us from transition. In many cases, it initiates it.

Post 5 — Clarity (Brand Essence · Alternate Pin)

Clarity rarely arrives through urgency. It arrives when pressure is removed long enough for perspective to surface. You don't need to reinvent yourself. You don't need to force the next step. You need to understand the moment you're standing in — and let the next step reveal itself naturally. That is the work we do.

Personal Forecast Session — Intake Questions

These questions filter without sounding like a filter. Include this opening statement verbatim:

"This work is reflective and advisory in nature. The questions below are designed to help determine whether a Personal Forecast Session is the right fit at this time. There are no 'right' answers — only honest ones."

  1. What prompted you to seek clarity at this moment? (Open field)
  2. Which of the following best describes your current experience?
    A. A quiet sense that something is shifting
    B. A specific decision I'm navigating
    C. A sense that a chapter is ending
    D. Questioning my direction or priorities
    E. Something else
  3. What feels most unsettled or unclear right now? (Open field)
  4. How do you typically approach decisions during uncertainty? (Open field) — Listen for: reflection vs urgency vs avoidance
  5. What are you hoping to gain from this session? (Open field) — Red flags: "answers," "certainty," "tell me what to do"
  6. This work focuses on understanding timing, cycles, and patterns — not predicting outcomes or telling you what to do. How does that land with you? — This is the strongest filter question.
  7. What are you most looking for right now?
    A. To understand the phase I'm in
    B. To slow down and get perspective
    C. To make a more informed decision
    D. To understand what's completing in my life
  8. Are you open to reflection without immediate resolution? (Yes/No)
  9. Have you worked with reflective, advisory, or insight-based work before? (Optional)
  10. Is there anything else important to share? (Open field)

Reading Responses

Green Flags

  • Thoughtful, measured responses
  • Language about timing, reflection, patterns
  • Curiosity without urgency
  • "I'm not sure what's next but something's shifting"

Red Flags (Decline Gracefully)

  • "I need answers now"
  • "I want to know exactly what to do"
  • Crisis language or emergency framing
  • Expectations of certainty or prediction

Decline Language (Keep Handy)

"Thank you for your thoughtful responses. Based on what you shared, it may not be the right moment for this particular work. I encourage you to seek support that better matches your current needs." (No explanation required.)

Intake Questions — Closing Statement

"This session is designed to provide clarity, context, and orientation. It is complete on its own and does not require ongoing engagement. If this resonates, you are likely a good fit."

FAQ — Personal Forecast Session

Use on the website, in email sequences, and as a pre-booking filter. Short, grounded, clear.

Q: What exactly is a Personal Forecast Session?

A one-on-one advisory conversation designed to help you understand where you are in your life cycle, especially during periods of transition. It focuses on timing, patterns, and context — so decisions can be made with greater clarity and less pressure.

Q: Is this coaching or therapy?

No. This work is advisory and reflective in nature. It is not therapy, counseling, or ongoing coaching, and it does not involve accountability or treatment. The session is complete on its own.

Q: Is this predictive or fortune-telling?

No. The session does not aim to predict specific outcomes or tell you what will happen. It helps illuminate timing, cycles, and themes that provide context for decision-making. The goal is clarity — not certainty.

Q: Do I need to be in crisis to do this?

Not at all. Many people come to this work simply because something feels like it's shifting — even if life appears stable on the surface. The session is often most helpful before decisions are forced.

Q: What kinds of topics can we explore?

Career and direction · Identity shifts · Leadership transitions · Life chapter changes · Pacing, timing, and priorities. You don't need a specific question prepared.

Q: Is this a one-time session or ongoing?

A Personal Forecast Session is a one-time engagement. Some people return at later transition points, but there is no expectation or requirement to continue.

Q: What happens after the session?

Most people leave with greater clarity, reduced urgency, a clearer sense of what is completing or emerging, and confidence in their own next steps. No follow-up obligation.

Q: How do I know if this is right for me?

If you value reflection, timing, and thoughtful perspective — and are open to clarity without being told what to do — this work is often a good fit. If you're seeking immediate answers or certainty, it may not be the right moment.

Q: Is this a replacement for financial, legal, or medical advice?

No. This work is advisory and educational in nature and is meant to complement — not replace — professional services.

Q: How do I begin?

Start with the $47 Personal Year Timing Report, or book a Personal Forecast Session directly through the Begin page. The report is the lowest-friction starting point.
