Should I Start a BusinessThis Year — or Wait?

The readiness question matters. But the timing question is what most business advisors never ask.

The direct answer: In Pythagorean numerology, Personal Years 1 and 8 are the strongest for business launches. A Personal Year 1 supports new beginnings and initiation — ideal for building from scratch. A Personal Year 8 is suited for high-ambition moves and financial focus. Personal Years 4 and 5 can also work: the 4 for building infrastructure quietly, the 5 for bold pivots when you're already positioned. Launching in a Personal Year 2, 7, or 9 tends to create more friction — those phases are structured for reflection, patience, and completion rather than aggressive external building.

Why Business Launch Timing Matters

Every business advisor asks the same questions: Do you have capital? Do you have a market? Do you have a plan? These are legitimate. But there's a question almost no one asks: Is this the right year for you, specifically, to launch?

Robert Schantz founded Human Behavior Specialists in January 2022 — a Personal Year 1 for the business and a deliberate timing choice. He has worked with clients who launched businesses at the wrong time and spent years grinding against invisible resistance, and others who waited one year and then had everything click quickly.

The difference isn't always skill, capital, or market fit. Sometimes it's phase.

Best Personal Years to Start a Business

Personal Year 1 — The Ideal Launch Year

The 1 year is the opening of a new 9-year cycle. Energy is initiating, forward-moving, and aligned with fresh identity. If you have been preparing a business idea and your 1 year is coming, this is the window to move. New ventures started in a 1 year have the natural momentum of the cycle behind them.

Personal Year 8 — The Ambition Year

The 8 year is about authority, results, and financial focus. If you have a well-developed concept and the infrastructure is already in place, a Personal Year 8 can produce significant traction. This year rewards high output and bold positioning. Not for tentative moves — for decisive ones.

Personal Year 5 — For Pivot-Style Launches

The 5 year is the year of disruption and change. It's not ideal for careful, methodical launches — but for someone who has been sitting on an idea too long and needs a push, the 5 year provides the energy to break out. Especially strong for unconventional or disruptive business models.

Years to Use for Preparation Instead of Launch

Personal Year 4 — Use this year to build infrastructure: systems, team, foundation. A 4-year business launch often stalls because the timing is better for internal construction than external visibility. Build now, launch in the 5 or 1.
Personal Year 7 — A year of inner work and research. If you're in a 7 year, this is when you get clear on what you actually want to build and why — not when you build it. Use this year for planning, testing, and market research.
Personal Year 9 — The completion year. Starting something new in a 9 year is like planting seeds in concrete. Much of what you build in a 9 year will either need to be rebuilt or will fall away before the 1 year begins. Use the 9 year to close what needs closing and position for what's coming.
Personal Year 2 — A patience year. If you launch in a 2 year, expect slower traction and more dependence on partnership and collaboration. Not a deal-breaker, but plan for it.

One Thing That Changes Everything

Knowing your Personal Year doesn't mean waiting passively. It means knowing what kind of action fits the moment. In a 4 year, build. In a 7 year, research. In a 9 year, let go. In a 1 year, move.

The business owners who use timing well don't waste energy. They do the right thing at the right time — and when the window opens, they're ready.
